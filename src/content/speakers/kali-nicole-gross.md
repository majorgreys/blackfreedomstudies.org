Kali Nicole Gross is a Professor of African American Studies at Emory University and the Martin Luther King, Jr. Professor of History at Rutgers University, New Brunswick. She is also the [National Publications Director for the Association of Black Women Historians, 2019-2021](http://www.abwh.org/), and a [Distinguished Lecturer](https://www.oah.org/lectures/lecturers/view/1524) for the Organization of American Historians. Her expertise and opinion pieces have been featured in press outlets including [*Vanity Fair*](https://www.vanityfair.com/style/2020/02/burning-down-the-house), [*TIME*](https://time.com/5786065/womens-history-month-women-to-know/), [*The Root*](https://theglowup.theroot.com/meghan-markle-s-choice-of-independence-and-self-respect-1841096725), [*BBC News*](http://www.bbc.com/news/magazine-35616529), [*Ebony*](http://www.ebony.com/news-views/the-criminal-unrapeability-of-black-women-405#axzz3lAjGcoaW), *HuffPo*, [*Warscapes*](http://www.warscapes.com/opinion/state-sanctioned-anti-black-violence-and-deadening-black-womanhood), [*The Washington Post*](https://www.washingtonpost.com/posteverything/wp/2015/10/01/black-women-are-obamas-most-loyal-voters-and-his-most-ignored-constituency/)*, and*[*Jet*](http://www.jetmag.com/life/in-response-to-the-charlestonshooting/#.Ve8ldmBU3ww). Her award-winning books include, [*Colored Amazons: Crime, Violence and Black Women in the City of Brotherly Love, 1880-1910*](http://www.amazon.com/Colored-Amazons-Violence-Brotherly-1880-1910/dp/0822337991/ref=tmm_pap_swatch_0?_encoding=UTF8&qid=&sr=), winner of the 2006 Leticia Woods Brown Memorial Book Prize, and, [*Hannah Mary Tabbs and the Disembodied Torso: A Tale of Race, Sex, and Violence in America*](https://www.amazon.com/Hannah-Mary-Tabbs-Disembodied-Torso/dp/0190241217)*,* [**winner of the 2017 Hurston/Wright Legacy Award for Nonfiction**](https://www.washingtonpost.com/local/the-underground-railroad-by-colson-whitehead-wins-2017-hurstonwright-award-for-fiction/2017/10/21/db756764-b611-11e7-9e58-e6288544af98_story.html?utm_term=.5087ad830074). Her latest book, co-authored with Daina Ramey Berry, is [*A Black Women’s History of the United States*](http://www.beacon.org/A-Black-Womens-History-of-the-United-States-P1524.aspx)*.*

Her numerous grants and fellowships include the prestigious Scholar-in-Residence at the Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture in 2007 and 2000, and the Ford Foundation Postdoctoral Fellowship, hosted at Princeton University in 2001-2002. She was selected to be a Public Voices Fellow for The Op-Ed Project, 2014-2015.
